The ball

It starts with a serve.

The ball flies in an arc and bounces once. If it lands outside the lines or bounces twice, the point is over. Every flight leaves its path on the court. For now nothing sends it back, so every point is one shot long.

Rule 1

Chase the bounce.

Wherever the ball lands, someone gets to it and sends it back. With no other rule, it goes back the way it came. The rally goes on and on, down one bright groove.

Rule 2

Hit where they aren't.

Each return goes to the side of the court that is empty. The ball starts to zigzag, pulling the rally out wide, and most points end after a few shots.

Rule 3

Go back to the middle.

After every shot, whoever hit it drifts back to the center of the baseline, so the next ball can always be reached. The zigzag locks into one figure and repeats almost forever.

Rule 4

Nobody is perfect.

Every shot misses its aim a little, and more when it was hard to reach. Balls start landing out. The perfect figure loosens into a weave, and no two rallies are the same.

Mini game

Your rally.

You hold the near side; the computer plays the far side by the same four rules. Reach the blue ring with yours before the ball gets there; catch it on the right of your ring to send it right, on the left to send it left. Every shot you trade stays on the court.

Ready to play?

  1. Move with the mouse or the arrow keys.by dragging left and right on the court.
  2. Serve with the Serve button, Space, or a click on the court.
  3. Return the ball by moving your yellow ring onto the blue ring before the ball gets there. The blue ring shows where to play it, just after the bounce, and turns yellow once your return is locked in.
  4. Win the point when the computer hits it out, into the net, or can’t reach your shot. Do the same and you lose.

Credits

Made by George Zhao for AI Design & Creativity at Cornell University, coded together with Claude. All code was written for this project.

  • The court uses the official ITF singles dimensions: 23.77 m by 8.23 m, with a 0.914 m net.
  • The ball follows simple projectile physics: gravity, one bounce that keeps 60% of its pace.
  • The players are never drawn. They exist only as the four rules above, and know nothing about tactics. In the mini game, you replace one of them.
  • The look borrows from long-exposure sports photography and ball-tracking graphics on TV.
